Assemblies get a bad reputation when they're treated as a one-off filler event. Done well, they can be a genuine turning point for students. Here's the process that tends to produce the best outcomes.

## Start with the outcome, not the date

Before contacting anyone, get clear on what the assembly needs to accomplish. Is it tied to a specific issue the school is navigating — bullying, mental health, a transition year? Is it meant to kick off a theme the school will build on for the rest of the term? A speaker chosen to match a clear goal lands very differently than one booked because a slot needed filling.

## Involve staff who know the student population

The best assemblies are shaped around the actual students in the room, not a generic audience. Share context with the speaker ahead of time: recent events at the school, the general mood, anything sensitive that should be handled carefully. A speaker who understands the room can adjust tone and emphasis without losing the core message.

## Budget for the whole experience, not just the fee

The speaker's fee is one line item. Travel, AV requirements, and the time needed for a proper sound check all affect how the day actually goes. Schools that build in buffer time for setup and a few minutes of connection with staff beforehand consistently get a smoother event.

## Plan the follow-up before the assembly happens

The energy an assembly creates fades fast if nothing follows it. Decide in advance how teachers will reference the talk in the days after — a classroom discussion, a related assignment, or simply a follow-up announcement. The schools that get the most lasting value treat the assembly as the start of a conversation, not the end of one.

## Ask about availability for smaller follow-up sessions

Some speakers, including workshop-based ones, can offer a smaller breakout session alongside the main assembly for a specific grade or group. This is worth asking about directly, since it's rarely advertised but can significantly extend the impact of the visit.
