Resilience isn't something young people either have or don't have — it's a skill that gets built through small, repeated experiences of facing a challenge and getting through it. Parents, educators, and community leaders all have a role to play in creating those experiences. Here are five practical ways to support that growth.

## 1. Let them struggle a little before stepping in

It's natural to want to remove every obstacle for a young person, but struggle — within reason — is where resilience actually develops. Give young people the space to work through a hard assignment, a difficult conversation, or a tough loss before jumping in to fix it for them.

## 2. Normalize setbacks as part of the process

Young people who believe setbacks mean failure give up quickly. Young people who understand that setbacks are a normal part of growth keep going. Talk openly about your own setbacks, and frame mistakes as information rather than verdicts on someone's worth.

## 3. Build a sense of belonging

Resilience is easier to sustain when young people feel connected to a team, a family, or a community that has their back. Mentorship programs, team sports, and community wellness initiatives all build this sense of belonging — and it's often the difference between a young person who gives up and one who pushes through.

## 4. Teach goal-setting as a skill, not a one-time event

Big goals can feel overwhelming without a plan. Helping young people break a large goal into smaller, achievable steps gives them regular wins to build momentum and confidence along the way.

## 5. Model healthy responses to adversity

Young people learn more from what they see than what they're told. When the adults around them respond to adversity with composure, honesty, and effort, that becomes the model they carry forward.

Building resilience is rarely about one big moment — it's about consistent, small investments over time.
