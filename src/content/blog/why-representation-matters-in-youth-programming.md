A message about resilience or ambition can be true and still fail to land. One of the biggest factors in whether it lands is whether the student sees themselves in the person delivering it.

## Representation changes how a message is received

A young person hearing "you can overcome this" from someone who looks nothing like them, grew up nowhere near their circumstances, and never faced the specific barriers they face will often file that message away as inspiring-but-irrelevant. The same message from someone who shares their background, community, or lived experience tends to be received as a real possibility rather than a platitude.

## It's not about matching every detail

Representation doesn't mean a speaker has to match a student's exact circumstances. It's about enough shared context — culture, community type, the general shape of the obstacles faced — that the student can draw a direct line from the speaker's story to their own.

## For Indigenous youth specifically

For Indigenous students, seeing an Indigenous speaker who has succeeded while staying connected to culture and community sends a message that's hard to deliver any other way: success doesn't require leaving who you are behind. That distinction matters enormously for students who've absorbed the opposite message, intentionally or not, from other parts of their environment.

## Representation in staff and programming, not just special events

A single assembly with a speaker who represents the student body is valuable, but it shouldn't be the only place that representation shows up. Schools and organizations that pair a speaker visit with ongoing representation in staff, programming, and daily environment get far more durable results than those treating it as a once-a-year event.

## What this means for organizations choosing a speaker

When selecting a speaker, think beyond topic fit. Consider whether the speaker's background gives your specific student population a genuine point of connection — and be honest about whether a generic, high-profile name actually serves your community better than someone with more direct relevance to it.
