Some of the clearest leadership lessons don't come from a textbook — they come from being part of a team that has to perform together, under pressure, with no guarantee of the outcome. The habits that make someone a good teammate are, almost without exception, the same habits that make someone a good leader at work.

## Accountability comes before authority

On a team, accountability isn't optional — if you don't do your job, everyone else feels it. The best leaders carry that same standard into the workplace: they hold themselves accountable first, and they earn the right to ask more of others by consistently delivering on their own commitments.

## Your success is not someone else's failure

High-performing teams are built on the idea that lifting up a teammate doesn't cost you anything — it makes the whole group stronger. Workplaces that build a genuine culture of supporting one another, rather than quietly competing against one another, consistently outperform those that don't.

## Adapt when the plan changes

No game goes exactly as planned, and neither does most work. Leaders who can adjust strategy mid-stream — without losing the team's confidence — are the ones who keep their group moving forward when circumstances change.

## Confidence is built through preparation, not given

Belief in yourself and your team isn't a personality trait; it's built through repetition, practice, and showing up consistently, especially when it's hard. Leaders who help their teams build real competence end up with teams that have real confidence.

## Communication under pressure

Teams that communicate clearly when things are going wrong — not just when things are going well — are the ones that recover fastest. The same is true of any organization navigating a difficult stretch.
