Booking a motivational speaker for a school assembly is a bigger decision than it looks. You have one shot at a packed gym full of students with a wide range of ages, attention spans, and life experiences — and you need a message that actually lands, not just a fun hour out of class. Here's what to look for.

## A story students can actually relate to

The strongest school assembly speakers don't lecture — they tell a real story students can see themselves in: a setback, a hard choice, a moment where things could have gone differently. Students tune out generic "follow your dreams" messaging quickly. They respond to specificity and honesty.

## Experience speaking to a wide age range

A speaker who can hold the attention of grade 4 students and grade 12 students in the same gym, often back to back, is a different skill than speaking to a single adult audience. Ask how the speaker typically adjusts their presentation for a K-12 or middle/high school crowd, and ask for references from schools with a similar student population to yours.

## A message that fits your school's current needs

Before booking, be clear about what your school actually needs this year — resilience after a hard year, anti-bullying and lateral violence awareness, mental health and wellness, or general motivation and goal-setting. The right speaker will ask about this upfront and tailor their presentation accordingly, rather than delivering the same talk to every school on their schedule.

## Follow-through, not just a one-time event

A single assembly can spark something, but the best engagements include some form of follow-through — a workshop format, materials for teachers, or a willingness to stay in touch with the school community. Ask what happens after the assembly ends.

## Logistics that respect your school's reality

Confirm technical requirements (microphone, screen, gym space), timing that works around your bell schedule, and whether the speaker is comfortable adapting on the fly if something changes the day of the event — schools rarely run exactly on schedule.
